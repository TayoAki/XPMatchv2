## 0. Distribution mode: intent-led

- **Why:** the traveler finds a plan at the moment of need, by searching "3 days in … itinerary", "how many days in …", "how to avoid tourist traps in …" (`validation.md`, search intent). Nobody follows a travel app's founder in order to plan a weekend.
- **What that means here:**
  - About 12 substantial assets over 90 days, ranked by how close the searcher is to planning *this* trip.
  - Each asset is a page on the landing site, plus spokes.
  - The founder's LinkedIn (`profile.md`) carries 1–2 spokes a week, not a daily calendar.
  - Forums and city communities get helpful answers, with no links where the rules ban promotion (RS does).
- **Unchanged from the default mode:** teach only, the matrix, trailer/meat/summary + CTC, curation, the hook bank and seasonal replay.

**Rules every asset follows (from AGENTS.md and the plan):**
- A real traveler's trip appears only with that traveler's permission **for web publication**. This is a separate scope from in-app matching (plan §7 `content_permission`). The contributor can withdraw it, and the page then drops it.
- No invented travelers, reviews, itineraries or statistics. An illustration is labeled as one.
- No traveler's dates, companions, spending or notes unless they chose to publish them.
- City assets wait for D-006 (the pilot city) and for at least 3 permissioned trips.

## Written posts

These are LinkedIn posts from the founder (spokes), with the meat written first. None uses a statistic, because none exists yet.

### Post 1 (asset 7): check an AI itinerary

**Trailer:**
> An AI can plan 3 days in a city in seconds.
> Checking it takes 10 minutes, and most people skip it.

**Meat:**
1. **Open every place's own website or map listing.** You're checking that it exists and is open on the day the plan puts it. Generated plans still suggest places that have closed.
2. **Put each day's stops on one map.** If a morning jumps across the city twice, the plan is ranking places, not planning your day.
3. **Count the famous names.** If every stop is on every top-10 list, you've got the same trip as everyone else who asked.
4. **Ask "why this, for me?" of three stops.** If the only answer is "it's popular", replace it.
5. **Find the catch.** Queues, cash only, closed Mondays, a 40-minute walk. If the plan names none, you'll meet them on the day.

**Summary:** Existence, geography, sameness, fit, catches. Five checks, about ten minutes, before you trust any generated plan.

**CTC:** What's the worst thing an AI itinerary has sent you to?

**Testable expectation:**
- **Stage:** problem-aware.
- **It landed if** comments name specific failures (closed places, cross-town zigzags).
- **It missed if** comments debate AI in general. Then Asset 7's page should lead with the checklist, not the argument.

### Post 2 (asset 8): the reviewer, not the stars

**Trailer:**
> A 4.8-star restaurant can still be wrong for you.
> The stars aren't the problem. Not knowing who gave them is.

**Meat:**
1. **Did they travel the way you do?** A 5★ from someone on a food pilgrimage means something different from a 5★ from someone who wanted a quick lunch.
2. **When did they go?** Menus, owners and prices change. Weigh last season over three years ago.
3. **What would they skip?** Good reviewers name the catch. A review with no catch is an advertisement or a mood.
4. **Would you have liked their other picks?** Scroll their other reviews. If you'd hate half of them, their 5★ tells you little.

**Summary:** Before trusting the stars, check four things about the reviewer: taste, timing, catches and track record.

**CTC:** Whose recommendations do you actually trust when you travel, and why them?

**Testable expectation:**
- **Stage:** problem-aware.
- **It landed if** answers name *people*, such as a friend, a specific creator or a partner, rather than platforms. That supports the "people like me" premise.
- **It missed if** most answers name a platform. Then T3 drops in rank.

### Post 3 (asset 9): the 4-slot day

**Trailer:**
> Short city trips fail at the scheduling, not the choosing of places.
> Here's the 4-slot day, which fixes that.

**Meat:**
1. **Give each day four slots:** morning, lunch, afternoon, dinner. Not eight. The gaps are where the trip happens.
2. **Keep each half-day in one area.** Pick the morning's neighborhood first, then find lunch within a short walk of it.
3. **Put the thing you'd hate to miss in a morning slot.** Mornings are least likely to be lost to queues and tiredness.
4. **Leave one slot a day loose.** Fill it on the day, or not at all.
5. **Check opening days per slot, not per place.** A café that's closed on Mondays is fine on Tuesday's plan.

**Summary:** Four slots, one area per half-day, the must-see in the morning, one loose slot, opening days checked per slot.

**CTC:** How many things do you plan per day on a city trip, and do you manage them all?

**Testable expectation:**
- **Stage:** problem-aware.
- **It landed if** people share their own number and their failures ("we planned 8 and did 3").
- **Why it matters:** the plan's slot design (D-036) assumes 4 slots a day. A consistent answer of 2–3 is a product signal worth passing to the planner's defaults.

### Post 4 (asset 11): share a trip so it helps

**Trailer:**
> Most trip reports are useless to the next traveler.
> Not because they're wrong, but because they leave out the one thing that helps.

**Meat:**
1. **Say who you are as a traveler:** pace, budget band, what you'd cross town for. A plan is only useful to people who travel like you.
2. **Give the order you did things in, and the area.** A list of places isn't a trip.
3. **Name one catch per stop.** "Worth it, but go before 11" is worth more than "amazing".
4. **Say what you'd skip next time.** It's the most useful line in any trip report and the one most people leave out.
5. **Leave out what's private.** Exact dates, who you were with, what you spent: the next traveler doesn't need them.

**Summary:** Your travel style, the order, a catch per stop, what you'd skip, and nothing private.

**CTC:** Been to [pilot city] recently? What would you tell the next person to skip?

**Testable expectation:**
- **Stage:** contributors.
- **It landed if** at least 3 people who've been to [pilot city] reply or DM. That's the supply signal that matters most (`idea.md`, binding constraint).
- **Before posting:** don't publish until D-006 fills the placeholder.
- **Measurement:** it's the active ask that `metrics.md` tests in W1. Post it on W1's first day, together with direct messages, and on no other date.
